About

Quality as an engineering discipline, not a phase at the end.

I've worked across several languages and stacks — most recently Python — and my focus is the part of testing that compounds: automation frameworks that stay maintainable as the codebase grows, and pipelines that give developers a clear answer within minutes of a push.

That means designing the test pyramid deliberately rather than accumulating UI tests until the suite is too slow to run. It means treating flakiness as a defect in its own right. And it means moving security testing into the pull request, where a finding costs an hour instead of a release cycle.

Capabilities

What I bring to a delivery team.

Not a tool list for its own sake — these are the areas where I take ownership of the outcome rather than just the execution.

Test strategy & architecture

Deciding what to test at which layer, and designing frameworks that survive team growth: shared utilities, stable selectors, deterministic fixtures, and a suite that stays fast enough to run on every commit.

CI/CD quality gates

Pipelines that block the right things and stay out of the way otherwise. Matrix builds, parallel shards, sensible caching, and reporting a developer can read without opening five tabs.

Containerised test infrastructure

Reproducible runs in Docker, ephemeral environments per branch, and test execution that behaves identically on a laptop and in CI.

Shift-left security

OWASP ZAP baselines in pull requests, dependency and container scanning, SAST/DAST integration, and findings triaged into something actionable rather than a wall of noise.

Performance testing

Baseline SLAs, peak and soak scenarios in k6 and JMeter, and throughput/latency regression tracked over time rather than measured once before launch.

Reporting & flake management

Unified Allure reporting per pipeline, trend charts, and systematic flaky-test tracking — because a suite nobody trusts is a suite nobody runs.

Approach

How I structure a test suite.

Each layer answers a different question. Getting the proportions right is what keeps a suite fast, trustworthy and cheap to maintain.

01

Unit

Fast feedback on logic correctness, run on every save. The widest layer, and the cheapest place to catch a defect.

PyTest · JUnit · TestNG
02

API & contract

Integration and contract checks across happy, edge and negative paths — most integration bugs are cheapest to catch here.

REST Assured · Postman / Newman
03

UI & mobile

Critical user journeys only, plus cross-browser and cross-device smoke tests. Deliberately narrow — this layer is the slowest and most brittle.

Playwright · Cypress · Selenium · Appium
04

Performance

Baseline SLAs, peak load and regression tracking on throughput and latency, run on a schedule rather than before launch.

k6 · JMeter
05

Security

ZAP baselines in pull requests, scheduled active scans against staging and pre-production, dependency and container scanning throughout.

OWASP ZAP · SAST / DAST
