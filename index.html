QA Assessment & Audits

Finding out what's actually wrong with a QA function.

Slow releases and escaped defects are symptoms. The useful question is which part of the system produces them — test design, pipeline architecture, ownership, or reporting that nobody reads. Assessing that honestly is work I've done repeatedly, most recently across an agile release train of five teams.

What an assessment covers

  • Test coverage and pyramid balance — where effort is misallocated
  • Automation health: flake rate, runtime, maintenance burden
  • CI/CD pipeline structure and where quality gates are missing or ignored
  • Defect flow — escape rate, triage discipline, root-cause follow-through
  • Test management tooling, traceability and requirement coverage
  • Ownership: who is accountable for quality, and whether they can act
  • Reporting and KPIs — whether the numbers drive any decision
01

Observe before recommending

Pipelines, test repositories, defect history and release records — plus conversations with the people who run them. Findings come from evidence, not from a maturity-model checklist.

02

Separate symptoms from causes

A slow regression suite and a high escape rate usually share one upstream cause. Fixing the symptom buys a sprint; fixing the cause changes the trajectory.

03

Prioritise by cost of delay

A ranked roadmap: what to fix first, what to stop doing, and what to leave alone. Sequenced so early wins fund the longer work.

04

Leave something operable

Reporting structures and KPIs the team keeps using after the assessment ends — the measure of success is that nobody needs a second audit.
